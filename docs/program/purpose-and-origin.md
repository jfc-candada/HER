# Purpose and Origin

## Goal 2

HER began as **Goal 2**: following hospital recovery, build an independent, voice-synthesized musical instrument.

The purpose was not to build an AI demonstration.

It was to restore a practical connection with music by creating an instrument capable of carrying technical complexity that could otherwise interfere with creative work.

HER therefore starts from the performer rather than from the technology.

## The human problem

Music is experienced as intent, feel, timing, tension, memory and response.

Those things do not arrive naturally as database fields.

A performer may know what they want without having a precise technical vocabulary for expressing it to software. A useful instrument must be able to bridge that gap.

HER explores that bridge.

It takes natural creative intent and progressively translates it into measurable evidence, musical relationships and executable performance material.

## The role of computation

The computational system is deliberately subordinate to the musical purpose.

HER uses local computation because the work requires:

- analysis of the actual audio collection
- semantic comparison between tracks
- signal processing
- musical reasoning
- persistent state
- rapid retrieval
- repeatable processing
- controlled experimentation

The technologies are means, not the program's identity.

Models may change. Hardware may change. Services may change.

The purpose does not.

## Human authority

The performer remains responsible for the creative decision.

HER can analyze, retrieve, calculate, compare, explain and propose.

It does not acquire artistic authority because it can perform those functions.

This distinction shapes the entire architecture.

## From Phase 1 to Phase 2

The first objective is to make creative intent actionable.

The next objective is to allow the instrument to learn from what the artist actually does with it.

That progression is described in [Phase 1 and Phase 2](phase-1-and-phase-2.md).
