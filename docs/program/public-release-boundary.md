# Public Release Boundary

HER's public repository is an intentional, curated record of the instrument's purpose, origin, creation and engineering development. It exists so that interested people can understand what HER is, follow its progress, inspect its demonstrated capabilities and engage with the project.

It is not intended to reproduce the complete private development environment or disclose every design and implementation decision. The public record is sanitised by design, but it should remain technically meaningful: preserving the project's history, evidence, major milestones and high-level engineering rationale is not the same as publishing the mechanisms that make its differentiated capabilities work.

## Publicly documented

The public project may describe:

- the musical objective, origin and development history of HER
- the problems and creative requirements that shaped the instrument
- Phase 1 and Phase 2 objectives, milestones and evidenced results
- externally observable capabilities and proof-of-concept outcomes
- major architectural responsibilities and high-level system boundaries
- general design principles, such as human authority, local-first operation and separation of authority from delegated computation
- development methodology, workstream organisation at an appropriate level, and qualification philosophy
- the existence of durable project knowledge, continuity and evidence practices
- future engineering direction, dependencies and openly stated maturity

These descriptions explain **what HER is, why it was created, what it has demonstrated and how the programme has evolved**. They should give a technically interested reader enough context to understand the project without providing a reproduction blueprint for its differentiated mechanisms.

## Retained privately

The private development environment retains material where disclosure could expose proprietary know-how, enable close replication, weaken system boundaries or reveal sensitive operational information. This includes, where applicable:

- source code and implementation-specific mechanisms
- differentiated architecture and design decisions beyond the public logical model
- detailed component decomposition, internal control flows and dependency relationships that expose how proprietary capabilities are assembled
- detailed interfaces, schemas, event models, state-transition rules and integration contracts where their combined disclosure would reveal implementation or decision mechanisms
- proprietary mathematical formulations, equations, feature construction and transformations
- parameterisation, weighting, ranking, selection and differentiated decision logic
- internal thresholds, tuning, calibration and evaluation criteria
- detailed retrieval, reasoning, orchestration and execution strategies
- implementation-specific prompt, agent, tool and control strategies
- proprietary data, curated datasets, annotations and derived material
- detailed operational topology, private network schematics, host-specific configuration and security controls
- private test evidence, forensic material, credentials, personal records and environment-specific information

A detail is not automatically safe to publish merely because it is not source code. Architecture, design rationale, interface definitions, data structures and workflows can collectively disclose valuable know-how. Their disclosure should be assessed by what a reader could reconstruct from the information, including when combined with other public material.

## The governing distinction

The public documentation should explain **what HER accomplishes, why it is designed as an instrument, and what evidence supports its stated capabilities** without unnecessarily disclosing **the proprietary architecture, design choices or mechanisms by which differentiated results are produced**.

This is not a rule to hide the existence of architecture or to make public descriptions vague. High-level responsibilities, system boundaries, project evolution and design principles are appropriate public engineering history. Detailed designs and combinations that would allow a reader to reproduce protected mechanisms remain controlled.

Capability and results can be demonstrated without publishing the complete implementation recipe. Where useful, public evidence should state the scope, conditions and maturity of a demonstration while withholding private operational or proprietary detail.

The public/private boundary applies to **code, architecture, design, data, workflows and operational knowledge**. It is part of the engineering discipline of the project, not an afterthought to publication.
